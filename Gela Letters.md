I went through a few ideas, but I’ll spare you the full chaos.  
 Jewelry crossed my mind, but I already did that years ago, and if I’m not inconspicuously measuring your finger yet, maybe that idea should rest.  
 There was a cardigan that looked very you, but the reviews were tragic and the shipping unreliable.  
 So I kept looking for something that actually made sense.

And I kept circling back to the same thing:  
 pens.  
 Specifically, *my* pens.

Then it expanded—pens became notebooks, notebooks became inks—because when I really thought about it, so much of my life with you is written.  
 All the letters, all the drafts, the 200+ pieces I’ve written for you or because of you—they all passed through a small set of tools.  
 These weren’t just stationery; they were the little machines my life ran through.

One pen wrote cathedrals of love for you.  
 Another dragged confessions out of me I had no business saying out loud.  
 The notebooks held the architecture of who I was becoming.  
 And the inks—  
 well, they were the tone of entire eras.

So I sat with that for a bit.  
 If there was anything of mine that felt right to give you,  
 it would be the things that lived all that with me.

That’s what these are.

The “fancy” one is for the sharp, deliberate parts of you—the entries you write when you need clarity, structure, or when the words feel a little heavier than usual.

The other is the workhorse, the one meant to keep up with you when your brain is spilling faster than your hand can follow.  
 The notebooks are for whichever version of you shows up:  
 the collected one,  
 the spiraling one,  
 the morning-after-reflection one.  
 And the inks—two colors, two moods—  
 are there so your words can look the way they feel.

I picked everything because they’re meant to be used,  
 refilled,  
 carried,  
 forgotten briefly,  
 rediscovered easily.  
 They’re tools you grow with, not tools you protect.

And because you journal like your life depends on it—  
 and because we’ve always lived so much of ourselves through words—  
 this felt like the only gift that made real sense.

So.  
 Here’s something of mine, for you.  
 For all the things you’ll write that I’ll never get to read.
