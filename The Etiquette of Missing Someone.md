#   
**The Etiquette of Missing Someone**  
  
  
One must begin with restraint.  
  
  
Under no circumstance is one to announce  
  
that they are missed.  
  
Such declarations are inelegant,  
  
and impose upon the absent party—  
  
who has already expressed  
  
a preference for absence.  
  
  
Correspondence is likewise discouraged.  
  
Draft nothing that requires sending.  
  
If words insist,  
  
they are to be folded neatly  
  
and kept out of reach—  
  
a private indulgence,  
  
never a social act.  
  
  
One must remain presentable.  
  
  
Carry yourself as though nothing  
  
has been misplaced.  
  
Attend to your duties.  
  
Reply when spoken to.  
  
Do not allow your gaze  
  
to linger where it is no longer returned.  
  
  
Recollection, when it intrudes,  
  
must be received with discipline.  
  
  
Do not revisit old conversations  
  
under the pretense of study.  
  
They are not texts,  
  
nor puzzles awaiting resolution.  
  
They have concluded.  
  
Permit them the dignity  
  
of remaining so.  
  
  
There will be moments—  
  
one must expect them.  
  
  
In such instances,  
  
allow only a measured concession.  
  
  
Stand still.  
  
Acknowledge the feeling.  
  
Do not name it.  
  
  
It sharpens when addressed.  
  
  
Above all,  
  
never presume reciprocity.  
  
  
To miss someone is, by nature,  
  
an unshared condition.  
  
It is neither invitation nor signal,  
  
nor patience in disguise.  
  
  
It is an act conducted in private,  
  
with as much grace  
  
as one can sustain.  
  
  
And if, despite every effort,  
  
composure should falter—  
  
if silence begins to resemble  
  
absence given form—  
  
  
recover.  
  
  
There are, after all,  
  
standards to be upheld.
